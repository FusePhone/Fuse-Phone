Interior Painting in Garden City Colonial Home

Garden City, NY • Interior Painting Project

← Back to Blog

Project Overview

Location Garden City, NY
Scope Walls, trim, doors, ceilings
Paint Used Benjamin Moore Regal Select (walls), Advance (trim)
Coats 2 coats walls, 1–2 coats trim
Timeline 3–5 days
Home Type Colonial

This colonial in Garden City had been painted years ago, and the wear was showing — hairline cracks along the plaster walls upstairs, yellowed trim in the hallways, and uneven sheen in the living and dining rooms. The homeowners wanted a complete refresh that would bring the home back to a polished, cohesive look without altering the character of the original millwork. Our team spent a full day on-site before any paint was opened, mapping out every surface and identifying where extra preparation would be needed.

Preparation & Surface Work

The upstairs bedrooms had original plaster walls with hairline cracking along the window returns and at several ceiling seams. We opened and patched each crack, then skim-coated the surrounding areas to create a flat, continuous surface. In the main hallway, years of paint buildup on the door casings and baseboard profiles had softened the detail — we sanded everything back to a clean profile before priming.

Throughout the first floor, we caulked every trim-to-wall joint, filled nail holes and minor dents, and spot-primed all repaired areas with a shellac-based primer to prevent bleed-through. The 9-foot ceilings in the living room required careful staging to reach the crown molding and upper wall areas safely and cleanly.

Application & Materials

Walls received two full coats of Benjamin Moore Regal Select in a matte finish — Balboa Mist (OC-27) in the main living areas, transitioning to a cooler Pale Oak (OC-20) in the upstairs bedrooms and hallway. The color shift between floors was handled with a clean break at the staircase landing, where the two tones meet at a natural architectural transition point.

All trim, doors, and window casings were finished with Benjamin Moore Advance in Chantilly Lace (OC-65), a waterborne alkyd that delivers a smooth enamel finish with strong durability. Crown molding throughout the first floor was hand-brushed for precision — no spray on the detailed profiles. The built-in bookshelves in the study were masked and painted separately to ensure sharp, clean edges against the wall color.

Final Results

The finished home has a clean, unified appearance. The plaster repairs are invisible under the finished coats, the trim profiles are sharp again, and the color transition between floors reads naturally. The homeowners noted that the built-in shelving and crown molding look like they were just installed — that’s the result of careful preparation and hand-brushed precision on every detail.
